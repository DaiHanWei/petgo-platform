# PRD: TailTopia Pet App — Automated Likes

## 0\. About this document

- **What this is:** the requirements for **automated likes**\. When a real user's post is 36 to 60 hours old, the job checks its likes once\. If it has fewer than 5, the post joins a stored **target set**, and a virtual account likes it once per run, at a random time in the 3 hours after the run starts, until it reaches 5 or the post is 7 days old\. Then it leaves the set for good\. Each author gets at most one automated like per run, and 10% of authors are held out as a control group\. Each like gives the owner the normal "someone liked your post" notification and push, which gives them a reason to come back to the app\.

- **Who it is for:** product \(Albar, Hex\), the backend developers \(Dai's team\) and QA\.

- **Release:** **backend\-only, as soon as possible** \(decision 2026\-11\-09\)\. There is no app change, no new screen and no new copy, so it doesn't wait for an app version\. Code goes through the usual flow: feature branch → the current `dev_<ver>` → `stag` → `main`\. It is switched on per environment by config \(FR\-221\)\.

- **Built on:** the existing **auto\-comment** job \(`content/autocomment`, live since 2026\-09\-29\), which posts one AI comment from a virtual account on posts that have no comments 2 hours after posting\. Automated likes reuses its schedule, its post filters and its virtual\-account picker\. Wherever this PRD says "same as auto\-comment", the auto\-comment code on `main` is the reference\.

- **Code baseline:** `petgo-platform` `main` at `4fed8dd6` \(V1\.4\.2 merged via PRs \#50–52, so topics are on `main`\) and `stag` at `13407f5a`, checked 2026\-11\-11\.

- **Numbering:** **FR\-212 to FR\-223** and **AC1 to AC50**\. The other V1\.5\.0 PRDs use numbers up to FR\-211 \(rule engine FR\-193, value pack FR\-194–195, pet species FR\-205–208, Feed double\-tap like FR\-209–211\), so this PRD starts at FR\-212\. Pet species later added its launch push as FR\-224, after this range. There are no admin \(AB\) items\. The only admin\-visible change is one row in the existing daily report \(FR\-220\)\.

- **Draft markers:** decided rules are plain body text\. `[ASSUMPTION]` marks something I inferred that Albar has not confirmed yet; §9 lists them all\. Open questions are in §8\.

## 1\. Why

TailTopia already gives a new poster one reason to come back: if nobody has commented 2 hours after posting, auto\-comment leaves a friendly comment, the owner gets a push, and they usually open the app the next day to see it and reply\.

After that, most posts go quiet\. A post that is a day and a half old with a handful of likes doesn't create any more reasons to open the app\. Automated likes fills that gap\. From that point the post keeps picking up likes, one per run and spread out, until it has 5\. Each like produces the normal like notification \("*kucingoren menyukai postinganmu*"\), so one quiet post brings the owner back several times over the next two days or so\.

The likes are meant to be a **return trigger**, not a vanity metric\. They must reach the owner's notifications, but they must not distort anything that ranks, recommends or measures real engagement \(FR\-218\)\.

## 2\. Users and journeys

### 2\.1 Who this is for

- **The post owner:** a real user who published a public post that didn't take off\. They get a few spaced\-out like notifications over the following days\.

- **Ops / product:** they read one daily number to see the job is working, and can turn it off per environment\.

Users who don't post, and posts by virtual or admin accounts, are out of scope\.

### 2\.2 Key user journeys

- **UJ\-1\. Sari's quiet post keeps calling her back\.**

    - **Persona \+ context:** Sari, 27, lives in Bekasi with her cat Mochi\. She posts a Moment once or twice a week, usually in the evening after work\.

    - **Entry state:** on Monday at 20:00 WIB she posts a photo of Mochi asleep in a laundry basket\. On Tuesday at 10:30 auto\-comment leaves a comment; she replies at lunch\. By Wednesday morning the post has 2 real likes and has dropped out of her feed\.

    - **Path:**

        1. Wednesday 10:30: this is the first run since the post turned 36 hours old \(it is now 38\.5 hours old\), so its likes are checked\. It has 2, fewer than 5, so it joins the target set\. The run plans its like for a random time before 13:30; at 11:47 a cat\-focused virtual account likes it\. Sari sees the push *"kucingoren menyukai postinganmu"* on her lock screen during a meeting and leaves it\.

        2. Wednesday 17:52 \(from the 16:30 run\): another automated like\. The first notification is still unread, so the second merges into it, and the push reads *"anabul\.lover dan 1 lainnya menyukai postinganmu"*\.

        3. On the way home she taps it and lands on the post: 4 likes\. She scrolls the feed for a few minutes\.

        4. Thursday around noon \(from the 10:30 run\): a third automated like brings the post to 5\. The post leaves the target set\.

    - **Climax:** across Wednesday and Thursday the same post pulled Sari back into the app twice, both times after its first day\.

    - **Resolution:** the post ends at 5 likes\. It never re\-enters the target set, even if a like is later removed\. On Friday she posts again\.

    - **Edge case:** a while ago Sari hid one virtual account after it commented on her post\. That account is never picked for her posts \(FR\-216\)\.

- **UJ\-2\. Ops checks it ran\.**

    - The next morning, ops reads the Lark daily report\. Under 社区, next to **自动评论成功数**, a new row **自动点赞成功数** shows how many automated likes went out yesterday\. If it reads 0 on a normal day, they check the config switch and the job log \(FR\-220, FR\-221\)\.

### 2\.3 Expected volume

Replayed against production data on 2026\-11\-11: every post by a real user whose check would have fallen between 2026\-10\-17 and 2026\-11\-10, using real likes only, the rules in this PRD, and each post's current status \(past review states aren't stored\)\.

- About **226 posts a day** reach their check\. **95%** have fewer than 5 likes then \(51% have none\), so about **215 posts a day from about 160 authors** join the target set\. In practice, almost every post by a real user gets topped up to 5\.

- With all the rules in this PRD \(per\-author limit and turn\-taking, FR\-214; 7\-day limit, FR\-215; 10% holdout, FR\-223\): about **660 automated likes a day** \(peak about 1,040\), reaching about **370 users a day**\. No user gets more than **2** a day\. Each of the 20 virtual accounts gives about **33** likes a day\. Apart from the launch run, one run plans at most about 570 likes, about one every 19 seconds over the 3\-hour spread window\.

- For comparison, without the per\-author limit it would be about 860 a day, and **15% of user\-days would get 4 or more** like pushes from the job\.

- **Launch run** \(checked for a launch at 10:30 on 2026\-11\-12\): **1,401** posts that are 36 hours to 7 days old have fewer than 5 likes, from **915** authors \(only 192 of them are 36–60 hours old\)\. After the 10% holdout, about 1,260 posts from about 820 authors join\. With the per\-author limit, the launch run itself plans about 820 likes, about one every 13 seconds\.

- The per\-author limit makes frequent posters' posts wait\. Without an age limit, the target set was still growing after 4 weeks \(about 1,300 posts\) and some likes landed on posts 24 days old\. With the 7\-day age limit \(FR\-215\) and turn\-taking \(FR\-214\), the set stays at about 600–800 posts and no like is planned for a post 7 days old or more\. About **20%** of posts that join leave at 7 days without reaching 5 likes, mostly frequent posters' posts\.

## 3\. Glossary

Every section uses these terms with exactly these meanings\.

- **Virtual account:** a user with `account_type = VIRTUAL` that is enabled, `status = ACTIVE` and not deleted\. In production there are **20**, user ids 1–20 \(Albar, 2026\-11\-10\)\.

- **Real post:** a post that is published, public and not deleted, and whose author is a real \(`account_type = REAL`\), normal \(`role = USER`\), not\-deleted user\. Text\-only posts count, and so do posts with images or video\. This is the auto\-comment filter **without** the image requirement\.

- **Under review:** a published post that has been reported and is waiting for moderation \(`UNDER_REVIEW`\)\. It is hidden for now, but may come back\.

- **Post age:** the time from the post's `created_at` to the start of the run\.

- **Like count:** the number of likes on the post at that moment, real and virtual together\. This is the number the app shows\.

- **Run:** one execution of the automated\-like job\. There are two a day, at 10:30 and 16:30 WIB\. At its start a run decides which posts get a like and plans each like for a random time in its **spread window**: the 3 hours after the run starts \(FR\-214\)\.

- **Holdout author:** a real user in the 10% control group\. Their posts never get automated likes \(FR\-223\)\.

- **Check window:** post age from 36 hours up to, but not including, 60 hours\. A 24\-hour window always contains exactly 2 runs\. The launch run alone uses a wider window \(FR\-213\)\.

- **Launch run:** the first run in an environment, recognised by the target\-set table having no rows at all, including posts that have already left \(FR\-221\)\.

- **Check:** the single moment a post's like count is looked at to decide whether it joins the target set \(FR\-213\)\. Every post is checked at most once\.

- **Target set:** the stored list of posts the job is currently liking\. A post joins it once \(FR\-213\) and leaves it once \(FR\-215, FR\-216, FR\-219\)\. A post that has left never comes back\.

- **Target:** 5 likes\. A post with 5 or more likes at its check doesn't join, and a post in the set leaves when it reaches 5\.

- **Automated like:** a like that this job created\. It is marked `is_automated = true` on its `content_likes` row \(FR\-218\), and has exactly one row in the automated\-like log\.

- **Automated\-like log:** the record of every like this job planned and what happened to it: post, virtual account, outcome and time \(FR\-219\)\.

## 4\. Features

### 4\.1 Building the target set

**Description:** a post is checked **once**, at the first run in its check window \(36 to 60 hours old\)\. If it has fewer than 5 likes then, it joins the target set and gets its first automated like in the same run\. If it has 5 or more, it is never selected\. The launch run is the one exception: it checks every post that is 36 hours to 7 days old, so recent posts that are already past 60 hours are taken in once \(decision 2026\-11\-11\)\. After that, a post that passes through its window without being checked \(because the job was off, or both runs failed\) is never checked \(decision 2026\-11\-11\)\. Once in the set, a post stays there until it leaves \(FR\-215\)\. Realizes UJ\-1\.

#### FR\-212: Schedule and run safety

The job runs at **10:30 and 16:30 WIB** \(Asia/Jakarta, set explicitly\), the same as auto\-comment\. Both times are configurable\.

- Only one run is active at a time, **even if more than one backend instance is running** \(for example during a rolling deploy\)\. If a run is still going when the next one is due, the next one is skipped and logged\.

- A post gets at most one automated like per run slot \(date plus morning or evening\)\.

- It runs independently of auto\-comment: if one job fails, the other still runs\.

**Consequences \(testable\):**

- **AC1\.** If the 10:30 run is still going at 16:30, the 16:30 run is skipped and the skip is logged\.

- **AC2\.** With two backend instances starting the same run, every post in the target set gets exactly one automated like for that slot, not two\.

- **AC3\.** If auto\-comment throws an error in a run, the automated\-like run at the same time still completes, and the reverse is also true\.

#### FR\-213: The check

At the start of every run, the job **checks** every post that meets all of these:

1. It is a real post \(§3\) and is not under review\.

2. Post age is in the check window \(≥ 36 hours and \< 60 hours\)\. On the launch run only: ≥ 36 hours and \< 7 days\.

3. It has never been checked before\.

At the check, a post with a like count **\< 5** joins the target set\. A post with **5 or more** is recorded as checked and is never selected\. A post by a holdout author is recorded as checked and never joins, whatever its like count \(FR\-223\)\. Either way, the post is never checked again\.

A post that is under review at its first run in the window isn't checked in that run\. If it is visible again by the second run in the window, it is checked then\. Otherwise it is never checked\.

**Consequences \(testable\):**

- **AC4\.** A public post by a real user, 40 hours old with 3 likes, is checked, joins the set and gets one automated like in the same run\.

- **AC5\.** A post 35 hours old isn't checked\. A post that is exactly 36 hours old at the start of the run is checked\. A post that is exactly 60 hours old isn't\.

- **AC6\.** A post with 5 or more likes at its check doesn't join, and is never checked again, even if its like count later drops below 5\.

- **AC7\.** Private posts, unpublished posts \(pending or rejected\), deleted posts, and posts by virtual or admin accounts are never checked\.

- **AC8\.** A text\-only post that meets rules 1 to 3 is checked\.

- **AC9\.** On the launch run, a post 50 hours old with 1 like joins, and so does a post 6 days old with 1 like\. A post 8 days old at the launch run is never checked\.

- **AC10\.** A post published 30 hours before the launch run isn't checked at launch\. It is checked in the first run after it turns 36 hours old\.

- **AC11\.** If the switch is turned off for 3 days some time after launch and then turned on again, the first run checks only posts that are 36–60 hours old at that moment\. Posts that passed through their window while the job was off are never checked\.

- **AC12\.** A post that is under review at its first run in the window, and visible again at its second, is checked at the second run\.

- **AC13\.** A post that has left the target set never joins again\.

#### FR\-214: Order and pacing

There is **no per\-run cap on posts**: every run plans a like for every post in the target set, except for the per\-author limit below \(decision 2026\-11\-11\)\. Posts are handled **oldest first** \(by `created_at`, then id\)\.

- **One per author per run:** each author gets **at most 1** automated like per run \(decision 2026\-11\-11\)\. If an author has several posts in the set, their posts **take turns**: the run likes the one whose last automated like is longest ago \(a post with none yet goes first; a tie goes to the older post\), so the owner isn't reminded of the same post every time \(decision 2026\-11\-11\)\. The others are skipped and stay in the set\. This caps any user at 2 automated likes \(and 2 like pushes from the job\) a day\.

- **Spread over 3 hours:** the run doesn't like posts one after another\. It gives each planned like a random time in the 3 hours after the run starts \(10:30–13:30 and 16:30–19:30\), so automated likes don't all arrive at the same minute every day \(decision 2026\-11\-11\)\. The re\-check in FR\-215 happens at that planned time\.

- **Planned likes are stored**, so a restart doesn't lose them\. If the backend is down at a like's planned time, the like goes out when it is back, as long as that is still inside the spread window\. Otherwise it is dropped for this slot \(`MISSED`, FR\-219\), and the post stays in the set\.

- **AC14\.** With 1,200 posts in the set from 1,200 different authors, every one of them gets its automated like from that run\.

- **AC15\.** Every automated like from the 10:30 run is created between 10:30 and 13:30, at random times\. The order of the likes doesn't follow the order of the posts\. No like from a run is created before the run starts\.

- **AC43\.** An author with 3 posts in the set gets exactly 1 automated like per run\. Over the next 3 runs each of the 3 posts gets one, before any of them gets a second\. A post that isn't liked in a run stays in the set\.

- **AC44\.** An author never gets more than 2 automated likes on one WIB calendar day\.

- **AC45\.** If the backend restarts at 11:00, likes planned for 10:30–11:00 that didn't go out are sent once it is back, provided that is before 13:30\. Likes planned for later go out at their planned times\. If it is down until 14:00, the unsent likes are logged `MISSED` and their posts stay in the set\.

\[ASSUMPTION A1: oldest first, to match auto\-comment's order\. It only matters for logs and ties; which of an author's posts is liked is set by the turn\-taking rule\.\]
\[ASSUMPTION A11: the spread window starts at the run time and lasts 3 hours\. Pushes stay far below the Tencent IM limit \(30 users per second for production and stag together; the backend holds itself to 25\): even the launch run in §2\.3 averages one like every 13 seconds\.\]

### 4\.2 Liking a post

**Description:** each post in the target set gets **at most one** automated like per run, from a virtual account picked the way auto\-comment picks one, until it has 5 likes or is 7 days old\. A post with no real likes, whose author has no other post in the set, needs 5 runs\. Because the gaps between runs alternate between 6 and 18 hours, that is about 48 hours from its first automated like \(give or take the 3\-hour spread\)\. Real likes get it there sooner; the per\-author limit can make it take longer, or stop it at 7 days short of 5\. Realizes UJ\-1\.

#### FR\-215: One like per run, leave at 5

For each post planned in a run, the job adds exactly one like from one virtual account\. At the planned time, just before liking, it checks the post again:

- **Deleted, rejected, made private, or author no longer real:** the post **leaves** the set with no like\.

- **Under review:** the post is **skipped** for this run and **stays** in the set\.

- **Like count already 5 or more** \(because of real likes\): the post **leaves** the set with no like\.

After the like, if the like count is 5 or more, the post **leaves** the set\. A like counts as an automated like only if the job actually created a new like\.

**7\-day limit:** at the start of each run, before planning, every post in the set that is **7 days old or more** leaves the set with no like, however many likes it has \(decision 2026\-11\-12\)\. No like is planned for a post 7 days old or more\. A like planned just before that can still land up to 3 hours after the post turns 7 days old\.

- **AC16\.** A post in the set with 2 likes ends the run with 3 likes and stays in the set\.

- **AC17\.** A post that joins with 0 likes, gets no real likes and is its author's only post in the set reaches 5 likes after 5 runs \(about 48 hours after its first automated like\), then leaves\. The job never takes a post above 5\.

- **AC18\.** If real users bring a post in the set up to 5 likes between runs, it leaves at the next run with no automated like\.

- **AC19\.** A post in the set that is under review is skipped and stays in the set\. If it is visible again at the next run, it gets its like then\. If it is removed by moderation, it leaves\.

- **AC20\.** A post in the set that is deleted or made private leaves at its next planned like, with no like\.

- **AC50\.** A post in the set with 3 likes that turns 7 days old at 09:00 leaves the set at the 10:30 run with no like, and is never liked by the job again\. A post that is 6 days 22 hours old at the start of a run can still get its like from that run\.

- **AC21\.** If the picked account already likes the post \(for example after a race\), no `LIKED` row is written and the like count doesn't change\.

#### FR\-216: Picking the virtual account

The job picks the account the same way as auto\-comment's `VirtualIdentityPicker`, with one more rule:

1. Leave out accounts the post's author has hidden \(blocked or reported\)\.

2. **Leave out accounts that have already liked this post\.**

3. If the post's species is known \(cat, dog or other\), prefer accounts set up for that species\. Otherwise use GENERAL accounts\. Otherwise use any account that is left\.

4. Pick at random within that group\.

Only virtual accounts in the §3 sense count, which means they must be ACTIVE\. A like from an account that isn't ACTIVE would produce a notification the owner can't see, and no push\.

- **AC22\.** A virtual account never likes the same post twice\.

- **AC23\.** An account the author has hidden is never picked for that author's posts\.

- **AC24\.** For a cat post where cat accounts are available, the account picked is a cat account\.

- **AC25\.** If no account is left **for this post** \(for example the author has hidden nearly all of them\), the post gets no like, is logged as `NO_IDENTITY` and **leaves** the set\. The run continues with the next post\.

- **AC26\.** If **no** virtual account is usable at all \(the whole pool is disabled, inactive or deleted\), the run stops before liking anything, logs why, and no post leaves the set\.

The account that auto\-commented on a post may also like it \(decision 2026\-11\-09: reuse the picker as is\)\.

With a pool of 20 accounts, a post can always find the up to 5 different likers it needs unless its author has hidden 16 or more of them\. Every account takes part in many posts' likes per run, so the same 20 names will appear across many users' likers lists\. This is accepted \(decision 2026\-11\-10\)\.

#### FR\-217: Notification and push

An automated like reaches the owner **exactly like a real like**\. It uses the same notification type \(`CONTENT_LIKED`\), the same rule that merges like notifications for a post while they are unread, the same 10\-minute push debounce per post, and the same copy \(`{0} menyukai postinganmu` / `{0} dan {1} lainnya menyukai postinganmu`\)\. The like is visible: it counts in the like count and shows in the post's list of likers, under the virtual account's name and avatar\. Checked in code on 2026\-11\-11: the notification code's checks for virtual or official accounts apply to the **recipient**, not to the liker, so virtual likers are notified normally, as auto\-comment's virtual commenters already are\.

- **AC27\.** After an automated like, the owner has a `CONTENT_LIKED` notification naming the virtual account, and gets a push if their like pushes are on\.

- **AC28\.** If the owner hasn't read the like notification from the previous run, the new like merges into it\. The time is refreshed, the badge doesn't increase again, and the push uses the merged copy \("… dan N lainnya …"\)\.

- **AC29\.** The owner's push settings and hide/block rules apply exactly as they do for real likes\.

There is no new copy, screen or notification type\.

### 4\.3 Keeping real\-engagement numbers clean

**Description:** an automated like is a nudge, not engagement\. For the owner it behaves like a real like, with one exception: it gives the author no growth points\. It must not count anywhere that ranks, recommends or measures engagement \(decisions 2026\-11\-09, 2026\-11\-11 and 2026\-11\-13\)\. Today `LikeService` publishes `ContentLikedEvent`, which four listeners pick up: growth points, pet milestones, @mention candidates and notifications\. V1\.5\.0 adds a fifth, for newbie task ② 获得第一个赞 \(首日与新手任务 PRD, its FR\-176\), which treats an automated like like any other like \(see below\)\. Separately, several queries count rows in `content_likes` with no virtual filter\.

To make this hold for future code too, every automated like is stored with **`content_likes.is_automated = true`**, and `ContentLikedEvent` carries the same flag \(decision 2026\-11\-11\)\. Every like created any other way, including likes ops makes by hand, has `false`\. Each exclusion below filters on this field\. Any new query that ranks, recommends or measures likes must filter on it as well\.

#### FR\-218: No growth points, and left out of rankings and engagement numbers

An automated like:

1. gives **no** growth points to the post author \(`LIKE_RECEIVED`\)\. Today this is **not** blocked: growth eligibility only looks at who receives the points, so a virtual liker would give the real author points and use up their daily quota for that rule\. The virtual account already gets no `LIKE_GIVEN` points, because virtual accounts can't earn growth\. This item covers only points for the like itself: if the like completes the first\-like milestone, the milestone's own points are still given \(see below\)\.

2. is **not** counted in the **home feed** interaction score, or in the periodic **P95** recalculation that the score is normalised against \(`首页推荐算法.md` §4\)\.

3. is **not** counted in **topic heat** \(`话题热度算法.md`\)\.

4. is **not** counted in the topic page's **"Populer"** sort\.

5. is **not** counted in **推荐关注 \(follow suggestions\)**, which ranks authors by likes received in the last 30 days\.

6. is **not** counted in **逛别人家的毛孩子 \(pet recommendation\)**, which ranks pets by likes on public Diary posts in the last 14 days\.

7. is **not** counted in the admin content list's **按点赞时间** ranking, or in the like number it shows in that mode\.

8. is **not** counted in the daily report's **点赞数** or like\-activity rate\. These already count only likes by real users, so this item is already met; it is listed so it isn't broken later\.

9. is **not** counted in **ops AI 查数** answers about likes or user activity\. The agent handbook \(`ai-query-agent/work/CLAUDE.md` §5\.2\) leaves out seed virtual authors, ids 1–20, but doesn't clearly say that likes they **give** are left out too\. Add one line saying so\. The rule matches on ids, so **any virtual account added later must be added to it too**\.

Items 1 to 7 apply to **automated likes only** \(`is_automated = true`\), not to every like by a virtual account\. Any like ops makes by hand from a virtual account keeps today's behaviour \(decision 2026\-11\-09\)\.

**What stays counted on purpose:** the like count the app shows, the post's list of likers, the "Suka" total on profiles, the like numbers in admin post detail, the pet's **first like** milestone \(`FIRST_LIKE`, with its notification, event and milestone growth points\), the author's @mention candidates \(author ↔ virtual account\), and V1\.5\.0 newbie task **② 获得第一个赞** \("get your first like"\), all of which an automated like triggers like any other like \(decisions 2026\-11\-12 and 2026\-11\-13; the 首日与新手任务 PRD needs no change\)\. The owner should see the likes; only rankings, recommendations, growth points for received likes and engagement measures ignore them\.

- **AC30\.** After an automated like, the author's growth ledger has no new `LIKE_RECEIVED` entry and their daily `LIKE_RECEIVED` quota is untouched\. Unless the like completes the first\-like milestone \(AC31\), their Tailrent level progress doesn't change\.

- **AC31\.** A pet whose first like is an automated like has the "first like" milestone completed by it, with the same notification, event and milestone growth points as a real like\.

- **AC32\.** After an automated like, the liking virtual account becomes an @mention candidate for the author, exactly as with a real like\.

- **AC33\.** Two posts that are the same in everything except 3 automated likes get the same home feed score and the same topic "Populer" position, contribute the same amount to topic heat and P95, rank the same in the admin 按点赞时间 list, and give their authors and pets the same standing in follow suggestions and pet recommendation\.

- **AC34\.** After an automated like, the like count in the app, the likers list and the profile "Suka" total all include it\.

- **AC42\.** A new user whose first like is an automated like has newbie task ② 获得第一个赞 completed by it, exactly as with a real like\.

- **AC46\.** Every like the job creates has `is_automated = true`\. A like ops makes by hand from a virtual account, and every like by a real user, has `false`\.

### 4\.4 Record keeping and reporting

#### FR\-219: Target set and automated\-like log

The target set is **stored in the database**, so a restart or a skipped run doesn't lose it\. Each entry records the post, when it was checked, whether it joined, when it left, and why: 5\+ likes at check \(never joined\), holdout author \(never joined\), reached 5, 7 days old, post gone, no account available, or too many failures\.

Every planned like writes one log row when the run plans it: post, run slot \(date plus morning or evening\) and planned time\. At the planned time the row gets the virtual account \(if one was picked\), the like count before the attempt, the outcome and the time\. Outcomes:

|Outcome|Meaning|Post stays in the set?|
|---|---|---|
|`PLANNED`|Planned, not yet attempted\.|Yes|
|`MISSED`|The planned time passed while the backend was down, and the spread window ended before it was back \(AC45\)\. Doesn't count as a failure\.|Yes|
|`LIKED`|A new like was created\.|Yes, unless the like count is now 5 or more|
|`REACHED_TARGET`|When checked just before liking, the post already had 5 or more likes \(AC18\)\.|No|
|`SKIPPED_REVIEW`|The post is under review \(AC19\)\.|Yes|
|`POST_GONE`|The post was deleted, rejected or made private, or its author is no longer a real user\.|No|
|`NO_IDENTITY`|No virtual account was available for this post \(AC25\)\.|No|
|`FAILED`|Any other error on this post\.|Yes\. It is tried again in the next run, and leaves after its 3rd `FAILED` attempt\.|

If the run itself is broken \(the whole pool is unusable, AC26, or 10 attempts in a row fail\), the run stops\. Its failures don't count towards any post's 3, and no post leaves the set because of it\.

- **AC35\.** The like and its `LIKED` row are saved together: an automated like always has a log row, and if writing the log fails, the like is rolled back\.

- **AC36\.** If one post fails, the rest of the run continues\.

- **AC37\.** A post with 3 `FAILED` attempts in runs that weren't stopped leaves the set and is never liked again by the job\.

- **AC38\.** If 10 attempts in a row fail \(for example because the database is down\), the run stops\. Every post that was in the set is still there at the next run, and their failure counts haven't changed\.

- The log holds only public information \(post id, account id, counts\)\. There is no post text\.

\[ASSUMPTION A8: the limit of 3 failures, so that a post that always errors doesn't stay in the set forever\.\]
\[ASSUMPTION A10: a run stops after 10 failures in a row\.\]

#### FR\-220: Daily report row

The Lark daily report gets a row **自动点赞成功数** in the 社区 section, straight after **自动评论成功数**\. It shows the number of `LIKED` log rows for that day \(WIB\) and, like the auto\-comment row, has no day\-on\-day comparison\. The card's footnote that explains 自动评论 gets a matching line for 自动点赞\.

- **AC39\.** On a day with 120 `LIKED` rows, the report shows 自动点赞成功数 = 120, and **点赞数** doesn't include those 120\.

\[ASSUMPTION A3: no day\-on\-day comparison, to match 自动评论成功数\.\]

### 4\.5 Switching it on and off

#### FR\-221: Config switch

A deployment\-level setting \(`petgo.auto-like.enabled`, env `AUTO_LIKE_ENABLED`\) turns the job on or off per environment\. There is no admin\-panel switch \(decision 2026\-11\-09\)\.

- **Deployed environments \(stag, production\):** **on by default**, like auto\-comment, so the job is active as soon as it is deployed\. Set `AUTO_LIKE_ENABLED=false` to stop it\.

- **Local and dev profiles:** **off by default**, so a developer machine pointed at real data never likes real posts or sends real pushes \(decision 2026\-11\-11\)\.

The **launch run** is the first run in an environment with the switch on, recognised by the target\-set table having no rows at all, including posts that have already left\. There is no other stored launch state\. Turning the switch off and on again later doesn't make a new launch run\.

- **AC40\.** With the switch off, runs do nothing except log that they were skipped\. After setting the switch and restarting, the change applies from the next run\. A backend started with the local or dev profile and no explicit setting doesn't like anything\.

#### FR\-222: Configurable parameters

Every number in this PRD is a setting with the value given here as its default: run times \(10:30 / 16:30\), the check window \(36h to 60h\), the maximum post age \(7 days, also the launch run's upper limit\), the target \(5\), likes per post per run \(1\), likes per author per run \(1\), the spread window \(3 hours\), the holdout share \(10%\), the failure limit per post \(3\) and the run\-stop limit \(10 failures in a row\)\. Changing any of them needs a config change and a restart\.

- **AC41\.** After changing the target from 5 to 4 and restarting, posts in the set leave at 4 likes from the next run, and new checks select posts with fewer than 4\.

### 4\.6 Control group

#### FR\-223: 10% holdout

**10% of real users** are holdout authors, so impact can be measured against users who don't get automated likes \(decision 2026\-11\-11\)\. Membership is decided by a hash of the user id with a fixed salt, so it never changes and needs no stored list\. It applies to authors only: a holdout author's posts are checked and recorded \(FR\-213\) but never join the set\. Everything else, including auto\-comment and likes ops makes by hand, works as usual for them\.

- **AC47\.** A holdout author's post that is 40 hours old with 0 likes is checked, recorded as "holdout author", and never gets an automated like\.

- **AC48\.** The same user is in the holdout group in every run, after a restart and after a deploy\. About 10% of real users are in it\.

- **AC49\.** The share of checked posts recorded as "holdout author" is about 10%, so the holdout and liked groups can be compared from the target set alone\.

## 5\. Non\-goals

- **Not a fake\-popularity feature\.** Automated likes don't push posts up the feed or into recommendations, don't give growth points for received likes or help with topics, and don't show up in engagement reporting \(FR\-218\)\.

- **No automated unlikes\.** The job never removes a like, including its own\.

- **No second round\.** A post that has left the target set, or was checked with 5\+ likes, is never liked by the job\.

- **No likes planned for posts 7 days old or more\.**

- **No catching up after launch\.** Apart from the launch run's 7\-day range, posts that pass through their check window while the job is off are never checked\.

- **No likes on comments**, and none on posts by non\-real authors\.

- **No new UI, copy or notification type\.** The like is indistinguishable from a real like in the app\.

- **No AI\.** Liking doesn't depend on reading the post\.

- **No admin panel page, toggle or log viewer\.** Ops uses the daily report row; developers use the log table\.

## 6\. Scope

### 6\.1 In scope

FR\-212 to FR\-223:

- a scheduled job that is safe across several instances;

- a single check per post in the 36–60h window \(36h to 7 days on the launch run\);

- a stored target set and one like per run until 5 or 7 days old, with no per\-run cap, at most one like per author per run, spread over 3 hours;

- a 10% holdout group of authors;

- the `content_likes.is_automated` field;

- the account picker;

- the normal notification and push;

- the growth\-point and ranking exclusions in FR\-218;

- the automated\-like log and the daily report row;

- the config switch \(on in deployed environments, off locally\) and the parameters\.

### 6\.2 Out of scope

- Any app release\. This ships backend\-only\.

- Changes to auto\-comment\.

- Changes to how real likes work\.

## 7\. Success metrics

Metrics compare the **liked group** with the **holdout group** \(FR\-223\) over the same period, starting at switch\-on in production\. The population is **posts by real users that were checked with fewer than 5 likes**: those that joined the set \(liked group\) and those recorded as "holdout author" with fewer than 5 likes \(holdout group\)\.

**Primary**

- **SM\-1: Return rate\.** For each such post, whether its owner opens the app at least once in the 48 hours after the post's check\. Reported as the share of posts, liked group minus holdout group\. No target yet: set it after the first 2 weeks live \(decision 2026\-11\-09\)\. Validates FR\-213, FR\-215, FR\-217\.

**Secondary**

- **SM\-2: Daily volume\.** 自动点赞成功数 per day, which should be steady and non\-zero once the job is on \(about 660 a day, §2\.3\)\. Validates FR\-212, FR\-220\.

**Counter\-metrics \(do not optimise\)\.** Product reviews these weekly for the first 4 weeks\. A clear rise is a reason to turn the job off\.

- **SM\-C1: Like\-push opt\-out\.** The rate at which users turn off like pushes, or all pushes, liked group against holdout group\. A rise means the nudges feel like spam\. Counterbalances SM\-1\.

- **SM\-C2: Hides and reports of virtual accounts\.** Against the 4 weeks before switch\-on\. A rise means users are spotting the pattern\. Counterbalances SM\-1\.

- **SM\-C3: `NO_IDENTITY` share in the log\.** If it grows, the virtual pool is too small or too widely hidden\.

\[ASSUMPTION A6: SM\-1 can be measured from the existing app\-open events\. These events are moving from PostHog to TailTopia's own server, which will hold and govern the data, so SM\-1 is read from there\.\]

A like\-push open rate isn't tracked: opening a like push sends no event today \(only ops pushes send `ops_notification_opened`\), and this PRD adds no tracking\.

## Appendix A\. Notes for developers

These describe the behaviour above in terms of the current code\. They are not extra requirements\.

- **Reuse:** model the job on `AutoCommentJob` / `AutoCommentService`: a cron with `zone = "Asia/Jakarta"`, an oldest\-first native query, and a transaction per planned like covering the re\-check, the like and the log row\. Reuse `VirtualIdentityPicker`\. Remove the post's existing likers from the pool before calling `pick`, and filter the pool to `status = ACTIVE`; auto\-comment's pool doesn't do that today\.

- **Single run across instances:** an `AtomicBoolean` only guards one server\. Use a database guard: an advisory lock or a lock row for the run, plus a unique key on \(post, run date, slot\) in the log\. A second instance then can't like the same post in the same slot\.

- **Target set:** a table such as `auto_like_targets`, with one row per `post_id` \(unique\) and the columns `checked_at`, `joined`, `left_at`, `leave_reason` and `failed_attempts`\. Posts checked with 5 or more likes get a row too, closed at once with the reason "5\+ likes at check"\. Each run does two things:

    1. Insert a row for every post due a check under FR\-213, using `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`\. If the table is empty, this is the launch run and the upper age limit is `max-post-age` \(7 days\) instead of 60 hours\. Then close every open row whose post is `max-post-age` old or more, with the reason "7 days old"\. Holdout authors' posts get a row closed at once with the reason "holdout author"\.

    2. Plan the open rows: oldest first, keep one open post per author, the one whose last `LIKED` row is oldest \(none counts as oldest; ties go to the older post, FR\-214\), and write one `PLANNED` log row per post with a random `planned_at` in the spread window\.

- **Sending planned likes:** a dispatcher, for example every minute under the same database guard, takes `PLANNED` rows whose `planned_at` has passed and does the re\-check, like and log update for each\. Rows still `PLANNED` when their spread window ends become `MISSED`\.

- **Holdout:** for example `hash(salt + user_id) mod 100 < 10`, with the salt in config\. Don't use the plain id, so the group isn't the users with ids ending in 0\.

- **Liking:** go through `LikeService.like`, so the like count, `ContentLikedEvent` and the notification path stay identical to a real like \(FR\-217\)\.

    - `LikeService.like` only checks that the post isn't deleted and is PUBLISHED\. The job must check PUBLIC, `UNDER_REVIEW` and the author itself\.

    - `LikeService.like` is idempotent\. Write `LIKED` only if a new like row was created \(AC21\)\.

    - Add the column `content_likes.is_automated boolean NOT NULL DEFAULT false` \(existing rows become `false`\)\. `LikeService.like` takes an `automated` argument that sets it, and `ContentLikedEvent` carries it\. Then `GrowthActivityListener.onLiked` skips it, while `MilestoneAutoCompleteListener.onContentLiked`, `MentionCandidateListener.onContentLiked` and `ContentNotifyListener.onContentLiked` handle it as usual \(FR\-218\)\.

    - V1\.5\.0's newbie\-task listener for task ② \(account record written from `ContentLikedEvent.authorId`, branch `feat/1.5.0-onboarding`\) does **not** skip it: an automated like completes task ② like any other like \(FR\-218, AC42\)\.

- **Queries to exclude automated likes from** \(FR\-218 items 2 to 7; all count `content_likes` with no virtual filter today; add `AND NOT is_automated`, which needs no join\):

    - the home feed interaction score;

    - `FeedRankInteractionStats` \(the P95 recalculation\);

    - topic heat;

    - `findTopicPopularIds`;

    - `UserFollowRepository.findSuggestionIds`;

    - `ContentPostRepository.findRecommendablePets`;

    - the admin content list's `countInWindow` in 按点赞时间 mode\.

- **Foreign keys:** deleting a post physically deletes its likes, so the log must not have a foreign key to `content_likes`\.

- **Notification behaviour already in place:** like notifications for a post merge while unread \(`LIKE:POST:{postId}`\), and the push is debounced by `notify:likepush:{postId}` for 600 seconds\. Runs are 6 or 18 hours apart and the spread window is 3 hours, so two automated likes on the same post are at least 3 hours apart, and every one gets its own push\.

- **Push rate:** Tencent IM offline push is limited per SDKAppID \(shared by stag and production\), and the backend caps it at `IM_PUSH_RATE_PER_SECOND` = 25\. Spreading a run over 3 hours \(FR\-214\) keeps the job far below that\.

- **Daily report:** add `SQL_AUTO_LIKES` next to `SQL_AUTO_COMMENTS` in `DailyReportQuery`, a `fieldNoChange("自动点赞成功数", …)` row in `DailyReportCard`, and a footnote line\.
